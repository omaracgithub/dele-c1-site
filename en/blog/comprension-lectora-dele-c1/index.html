Reading Comprehension DELE C1: Strategies

Updated: June 2026

The reading comprehension and language use test of the DELE C1 is the longest part of the exam: 90 minutes for 5 tasks and 40 items. Time management is crucial, and you need specific strategies for each type of task. In this article, we explain how to approach each one efficiently.

Time Distribution

With 90 minutes for 5 tasks, the recommended distribution is:

  • Task 1: 15-18 minutes
  • Task 2: 18-20 minutes
  • Task 3: 15-18 minutes
  • Task 4: 18-20 minutes
  • Task 5: 12-15 minutes
  • Review: Final 5 minutes

Bring a watch and keep track of the time. If a task is taking too long, mark your best options and move on. It’s better to answer all tasks than to leave any blank.

Task 1: Global Comprehension

Format: A long text (600-700 words) of journalistic, essayistic, or informative type. 6 multiple-choice questions (3 options) about main ideas, author’s intention, and global comprehension.

Strategies

  • Read the questions first: This way, you’ll know what to look for in the text.
  • First quick reading: Identify the topic, thesis, and general structure (2-3 minutes).
  • Second directed reading: Look for the specific information you need for each question.
  • Locate the evidence: For each answer, identify the paragraph or sentence that justifies it.
  • Be careful with paraphrases: The correct answer rarely uses the same words as the text; look for synonyms and reformulations.

Task 2: Text Reconstruction

Format: A text from which 6 fragments have been extracted. You must place them back in their correct position. There is one extra fragment that does not correspond to any gap (7 options for 6 gaps).

Strategies

  • Read the complete text first: Understand the topic and logical progression before trying to fill in gaps.
  • Look for grammatical clues: Pronouns (this, that, which), connectors (however, moreover), verb tenses that must agree.
  • Theme coherence: The fragment must continue the theme of the previous paragraph and connect with the next one.
  • Start with the clearest ones: Some gaps have very obvious clues; solve those first.
  • Check for cohesion: Once a fragment is placed, reread the complete paragraph to confirm it flows naturally.
  • The leftover fragment: It is often a distractor that addresses a similar topic but does not fit grammatically or thematically.

Task 3: Detailed Comprehension

Format: An academic or specialized text (500-600 words). 6 multiple-choice questions about detailed information, inferences, and the meaning of expressions in context.

Strategies

  • Pay attention to nuances: C1 questions assess implicit understanding, not just explicit.
  • Inference: Ask yourself, “What does the author really mean?” beyond the literal words.
  • Vocabulary in context: If asked about the meaning of an expression, reread the entire paragraph to grasp the sense.
  • Eliminate options: Discard clearly incorrect options before choosing from the remaining ones.
  • Do not add information: The answer must be justified by the text, not by your prior knowledge of the topic.

Task 4: Information Location

Format: Several short texts (3-6 texts) on a common topic but with different perspectives. You must identify in which text each piece of information or idea appears (8-10 items). Some texts may correspond to more than one item.

Strategies

  • Read the statements first: Understand what information you need to locate.
  • Read each text and mark: As you read each text, note which statements might correspond to it.
  • Look for synonyms: The statements reformulate the ideas of the texts with different words.
  • Attention to opinions vs. facts: If the statement says “the author criticizes...,” look for a text with a critical tone.
  • Do not confuse texts: Two texts may discuss the same subtopic but with opposing views.

Task 5: Language Use

Format: A text with 14 gaps. For each gap, you choose the correct word or expression from 3 options. Grammar, vocabulary, collocations, and fixed expressions are evaluated.

Strategies

  • Read the complete text first: Understand the general context before solving the gaps.
  • Analyze the type of gap: Is it a preposition? A connector? A collocation? A verb with a prepositional regime?
  • Collocations: Many items evaluate fixed combinations (take measures, not *adopt measures; pay attention, not *give attention).
  • Prepositions: They are common: “depend on,” “consist of,” “lack,” “count on.”
  • Connectors: Identify the logical relationship (cause, consequence, contrast, addition) to choose the correct one.
  • If in doubt, read aloud: Sometimes your ear helps you identify what “sounds right.”

General Inference Techniques

Inference is key in DELE C1. Practice these techniques:

  • Identify the tone: Is the author objective, critical, ironic, enthusiastic?
  • Look for the implicit thesis: What does the author defend even if not explicitly stated?
  • Connect ideas between paragraphs: How does each paragraph relate to the previous one?
  • Pay attention to examples: What general idea do they illustrate?
  • Attitude markers: Words like “unfortunately,” “surprisingly,” “it is to be expected” reveal the author's stance.

Vocabulary in Context: How to Deduce Meanings

When you encounter an unknown word:

  1. Analyze the root: Do you recognize any prefix or suffix? (dis-employment, un-sustainable, re-think)
  2. Look at the immediate context: The words surrounding the unknown often provide clues.
  3. Look for definitions in the text: Sometimes the author explains the term in commas or parentheses.
  4. Identify the grammatical function: Is it a noun, verb, adjective? This reduces the possibilities.
  5. Use the logic of the argument: What sense would the sentence make if the word meant X?

Common Mistakes in Reading Comprehension

  • Reading too slowly: You don’t need to understand every word; look for relevant information.
  • Choosing the first option that “sounds good”: Always read all three options before deciding.
  • Being influenced by prior knowledge: Respond according to the text, not based on what you know about the topic.
  • Not managing time: Getting stuck on one task and not reaching the next ones.
