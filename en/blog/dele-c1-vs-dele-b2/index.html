DELE C1 vs DELE B2: differences and which one to choose

Updated: June 2026

If you already have an upper-intermediate level of Spanish, you are probably wondering whether you should take the DELE B2 or make the leap to the DELE C1. Both are official diplomas issued by the Instituto Cervantes, but they certify different levels of competence. In this article, we analyze the key differences to help you make the best decision.

Level difference according to the CEFR

The Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR) clearly defines the difference between both levels:

  • B2 (Independent user advanced): You can understand the main ideas of complex texts, interact with native speakers fluently, and produce clear and detailed texts on various topics.
  • C1 (Proficient user): You can understand extensive and demanding texts with implicit meanings, express yourself fluently and spontaneously, and use the language flexibly and effectively for academic and professional purposes.

The fundamental difference is that C1 requires capturing nuances, irony, implicit meanings, and managing formal registers with precision, something that is not evaluated with the same depth in B2.

Exam structure: B2 vs C1

DELE B2

  • Reading comprehension: 70 min, 4 tasks, 36 items
  • Listening comprehension: 40 min, 5 tasks, 30 items
  • Written expression and interaction: 80 min, 2 tasks
  • Oral expression and interaction: 20 min preparation + 20 min exam, 3 tasks

DELE C1

  • Reading comprehension and language use: 90 min, 5 tasks, 40 items
  • Listening comprehension and language use: 50 min, 5 tasks, 30 items
  • Integrated skills — Listening comprehension + written expression: 80 min, 2 tasks
  • Integrated skills — Reading comprehension + oral expression: 20 min preparation + 20 min exam, 3 tasks

The most important structural difference is that in C1, the production tests (writing and oral) are integrated with the comprehension tests. This means that to write or speak, you must first understand a text or audio and then produce from it.

Difficulty: how much harder is C1?

The jump from B2 to C1 is significant. Here are the main differences in difficulty:

  • Longer and more complex texts: C1 texts are longer, with more specialized vocabulary and more elaborate syntactic structures.
  • Implicit comprehension: C1 evaluates the ability to infer non-explicit information, grasp irony, and understand cultural references.
  • Lexical precision: A wider and more precise vocabulary is expected, including idiomatic expressions, collocations, and formal register.
  • Integrated production: Writing and oral tasks require processing input information before producing, which adds cognitive complexity.
  • Time management: With more tasks and longer texts, the time pressure is greater.

When to choose DELE B2?

DELE B2 is the best option if:

  • You need a certificate for university access (many universities accept B2)
  • Your current level is between B1+ and B2
  • You need the certificate in the short term and do not have time to prepare for C1
  • Your goal is to demonstrate general communicative competence without the need for an advanced level
  • You work in an environment where Spanish is not your main tool

When to choose DELE C1?

DELE C1 is the best option if:

  • You need to certify an advanced level to practice a regulated profession in Spain
  • You aspire to positions in international organizations (UN, EU)
  • You want to access competitive postgraduate programs
  • Your current level is already a solid B2+
  • You use Spanish daily in professional or academic contexts
  • You want to stand out in the job market

Scoring system

Both exams have a maximum score of 100 points divided into two groups of 50. In both cases, you need a minimum of 30 points in each group to pass. The difference is that the level of demand of the tasks is higher in C1.

Preparation time

If you start from a consolidated B2 level, you will need approximately:

  • For DELE B2: 2-3 months of specific preparation
  • For DELE C1: 4-6 months of intensive preparation

Our recommendation

If you have doubts, take a practice exam for C1. If you score at least 50% on the comprehension tests, you can probably prepare for C1 in a few months. If you are below that, first consolidate your B2 and consider C1 as a medium-term goal.

Remember that the DELE does not expire: once passed, it is yours forever. So it is worth investing the necessary time to take the level that truly reflects your competence.
