Common mistakes in the DELE C1 and how to avoid them

Updated: June 2026

Many well-prepared candidates fail the DELE C1 not due to a lack of level, but because of avoidable mistakes: poor time management, not reading the instructions carefully, or not adapting the register to the type of text. In this article, we review the most frequent mistakes in each test and provide you with concrete strategies to avoid them.

General mistakes (all tests)

1. Not reading the complete instructions

It seems obvious, but under the pressure of the exam, many candidates start answering without properly reading what is being asked. Each task has specific instructions that determine exactly what you need to do.

Solution: Spend 30 seconds reading the instructions for each task, even if you already know them from practice. Highlight the key words.

2. Poor time management

Getting stuck on one task and not completing the following ones is one of the most costly mistakes. Remember: an unanswered task is a guaranteed 0.

Solution: Bring a watch and assign a maximum time to each task. If time runs out, mark your best option and move on.

3. Not knowing the exam format

Showing up without knowing the exact structure of the exam (4 tests, 2 groups, minimum score per group) generates unnecessary anxiety and strategic errors.

Solution: Do at least 2-3 complete mock exams before the real exam. Familiarize yourself with each type of task.

4. Neglecting a whole group

Some candidates focus only on comprehension (Group 1) and neglect production (Group 2), or vice versa. You need a minimum of 30/50 in each group.

Solution: Practice all 4 tests in a balanced way. If one is your weak point, dedicate more time to it, but do not abandon the others.

Reading comprehension mistakes

5. Reading the entire text before the questions

Reading a long text of 700 words without knowing what to look for is inefficient. By the time you get to the questions, you have already forgotten details.

Solution: Read the questions first, then the text looking for the answers.

6. Choosing answers based on prior knowledge

The correct answer is the one justified by the text, not the one you know to be true in real life.

Solution: For each answer, identify the sentence or paragraph from the text that supports it.

7. Not using elimination

In multiple choice with 3 options, eliminating one incorrect answer gives you a 50% chance of getting it right.

Solution: Always first discard the clearly incorrect options.

Listening comprehension mistakes

8. Trying to understand every word

In C1, the audios are fast and contain specialized vocabulary. You do not need to understand 100% to answer correctly.

Solution: Focus on the main ideas and the information you need for the questions.

9. Not taking advantage of the second listening

Some candidates relax during the second listening because they have already answered. But the second listening is your opportunity to verify and correct.

Solution: In the first listening, answer what you can and mark your doubts. In the second, confirm and complete.

10. Not reading the questions before the audio

If you do not know what to look for, the audio plays and you lose crucial information.

Solution: Use the time between tasks to read the questions for the next one. Highlight key words.

Writing expression mistakes

11. Not respecting the type of text

If you are asked for a formal letter and write an essay, or if you are asked for a report and write a letter, you lose points for appropriateness even if your Spanish is perfect.

Solution: Clearly identify what type of text is being requested and respect its structure and register.

12. Not respecting the length

Writing 150 words when 220-250 are requested, or writing 350, incurs penalties. Evaluators count.

Solution: Practice writing texts of exactly 220-250 words. Learn to estimate your length by lines.

13. Lack of connectors

A text without connectors looks like a list of loose ideas. In C1, advanced textual cohesion is expected.

Solution: Use at least 6-8 varied connectors per text. Do not always repeat "furthermore" and "however."

14. Not planning before writing

Starting to write without an outline leads to disorganized, repetitive, or incomplete texts.

Solution: Spend 3-5 minutes making an outline before writing. Decide on the structure and main points.

Oral expression mistakes

15. Reading notes aloud

Examiners immediately detect when a candidate reads instead of speaking. This is severely penalized.

Solution: Write only key words in your notes, never complete sentences. Use the notes as a visual guide, not as a script.

16. Not interacting in tasks 2 and 3

Some candidates make monologues even in the debate and negotiation. Your ability to interact is evaluated.

Solution: Listen to the examiner, respond to what they say, ask questions, show that it is a real conversation.

17. Responses that are too short

Responding with one sentence when a development of several minutes is expected shows a lack of fluency.

Solution: Develop your ideas: give your opinion, justify it, provide an example, and conclude.

18. Filler words and excessive pauses

"Uh...", "well...", "so...", "I mean..." in excess detract from fluency and give an impression of insecurity.

Solution: Record yourself practicing and count your filler words. Replace them with brief and silent pauses.

Pre-exam checklist

Before the exam day, confirm that:

  • You know the exact structure of the 4 tests
  • You have done at least 2 complete timed mock exams
  • You know how much time to dedicate to each task
  • You are familiar with the types of texts that may be requested in writing
  • You have practiced speaking with a timer (20 min prep + 20 min)
  • You have a repertoire of varied connectors
  • You bring a watch, pen, and documentation
