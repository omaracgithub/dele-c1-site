DELE C1 Oral Expression: The 3 Tasks Explained

Updated: June 2026

The reading comprehension and oral expression and interaction test of the DELE C1 is the last part of the exam and is part of Group 2 (integrated skills). You have 20 minutes of preparation (with supporting material) followed by 20 minutes of examination in front of two examiners. It consists of 3 tasks that evaluate your ability to present, debate, and negotiate in Spanish.

General Structure

  • Preparation: 20 minutes in a separate room with the materials for the 3 tasks. You can take notes.
  • Exam: 20 minutes in front of two examiners (one interacts with you, the other evaluates).
  • Task 1: Monologue (~5 minutes)
  • Task 2: Debate (~5-6 minutes)
  • Task 3: Negotiation (~5-6 minutes)

Task 1: Monologue (presentation of a topic)

Format: You receive a short text (article, graphic, data) on a topic. You must make an oral presentation of 3-5 minutes where you summarize the information, give your opinion, and develop the topic.

How to Prepare It (in 20 min)

  1. Read the text carefully (2-3 min)
  2. Identify the main ideas and your stance
  3. Make an outline: introduction → development (2-3 points) → conclusion
  4. Note down key words and connectors you want to use
  5. DO NOT write a complete text to read; just guiding notes

Recommended Structure

  1. Introduction: Present the topic and why it is relevant
  2. Summary of the text: Briefly mention the main ideas of the material
  3. Development: Expand with your opinion, examples, additional data
  4. Conclusion: Summarize your stance and close

Useful Phrases

  • “The text provided to me addresses the topic of...”
  • “According to the presented data, it can be observed that...”
  • “In my opinion, this phenomenon is due to...”
  • “I would like to add that, from my experience...”
  • “In conclusion, I believe that...”

Task 2: Debate (conversation with the examiner)

Format: The examiner takes a stance contrary to yours on the topic of Task 1. You must defend your opinion, counter-argue, and maintain a fluid conversation.

How to Prepare It

  • Anticipate possible counterarguments to your stance
  • Prepare data or examples to reinforce your position
  • Think of concessions you can make without abandoning your thesis

Strategies During the Debate

  • Listen actively: Do not interrupt; wait for the examiner to finish
  • Rephrase before responding: “If I understood you correctly, you argue that...”
  • Partially concede: “It is true that..., however...” shows argumentative maturity
  • Use concrete examples: Illustrate your arguments with real or hypothetical cases
  • Stay calm: It is an academic debate, not a personal discussion

Useful Phrases for Debating

  • “I understand your point of view, but I believe that...”
  • “Allow me to disagree on this point...”
  • “While it is true that..., we cannot ignore that...”
  • “That argument precisely reinforces my position, since...”
  • “That statement needs to be qualified, since...”
  • “From another perspective, we could argue that...”

Task 3: Negotiation

Format: You are presented with a situation in which you must reach an agreement with the examiner. For example: organizing an event, resolving a neighborhood conflict, planning a project. Each person has a role with different interests.

How to Prepare It

  • Read your role and your “interests” or “conditions” carefully
  • Identify which points are negotiable and which are not
  • Think of compromise proposals
  • Prepare alternatives: “If X is not possible, we could...”

Negotiation Strategies

  • Start by stating your position: Make it clear what you need or want
  • Ask the other person: “What would you think if...?” “Would you be willing to...?”
  • Propose alternatives: Show flexibility by offering options
  • Seek agreement: The goal is to reach a solution, not to “win”
  • Summarize agreements: “So, we agreed that...”

Useful Phrases for Negotiating

  • “My proposal would be the following...”
  • “What would you think if instead of that...?”
  • “I would be willing to accept... as long as...”
  • “We could reach a middle ground if...”
  • “I’m afraid that is not possible for me, but as an alternative...”
  • “I believe we have found a satisfactory solution for both.”

How to Use the 20 Minutes of Preparation

The recommended distribution:

  • Minutes 1-7: Task 1 — Read the text, make your presentation outline
  • Minutes 8-12: Task 2 — Anticipate counterarguments, prepare rebuttals
  • Minutes 13-18: Task 3 — Read the situation, identify your interests, think of proposals
  • Minutes 19-20: Quick review of notes and key vocabulary

Body Language and Non-Verbal Communication

  • Eye contact: Look at the examiners, not at your notes constantly
  • Open posture: Sit up straight, do not cross your arms
  • Natural gestures: Use your hands to emphasize, but without exaggerating
  • Smile: Show that you are comfortable and enjoying the conversation
  • Rhythm: Do not speak too fast or too slow; make natural pauses

Common Mistakes in Oral Expression

  • Reading from notes: Notes are a guide, not a script. Speak naturally.
  • Too short monologue: If you finish in 2 minutes, you haven't developed enough.
  • Not interacting: In tasks 2 and 3, you must listen and respond, not just speak.
  • Excessive fillers: “Um...”, “well...”, “I mean...” in excess detracts from fluency.
  • Poor vocabulary: Use synonyms, avoid repeating the same words.
  • Not concluding: Always close your intervention with a clear conclusion.

Evaluation Criteria

The examiners assess:

  • Coherence and fluency: Is your speech organized and fluid?
  • Grammatical accuracy: Do you make errors that hinder understanding?
  • Lexical range: Do you use varied and precise vocabulary?
  • Interaction: Do you respond adequately? Do you maintain the conversation?
  • Pronunciation and intonation: Is your speech clear? Is your intonation natural?
